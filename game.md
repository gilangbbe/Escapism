The Last Voyage of the Black Vesper
A 2D cooperative escape room scenario for 5 players.
Premise
The year is 1721. The players are senior crew of the pirate brig Black Vesper, the most feared ship in the Caribbean. Captain Mordecai Vane has betrayed them. After a successful raid on the Spanish galleon San Cristóbal, he drugged the rum, locked the crew in the brig, and is now sailing the Vesper toward Port Royal — where he has struck a deal with the British Navy to trade the crew's lives for a royal pardon and the San Cristóbal's gold.
It is currently 4:30 AM. The ship will reach Port Royal harbor at 6:00 AM, where the Navy is waiting. The crew must escape the brig, retake the ship, find the hidden ledger that proves Vane's betrayal (so they can claim their innocence later if captured), and steer the Vesper away from the trap — all before sunrise.
Win conditions (best ending): Escape the brig, defeat or convert the loyalists, recover the ledger, and change course before 6:00 AM. Time limit: 90 minutes of real time. Failure states: Time expires (Navy boards), the ship runs aground (a midgame timer that can be reset), or the crew is detected too early by Vane's loyalists (alarm meter).
Player Roles
Each role starts with a unique inventory and ability. Roles are not just flavor — many puzzles require a specific role to interact, which forces the group to communicate and rotate who's at the front.
Mira "Ironhand" Castellanos — The Quartermaster. Starts with a worn ledger fragment and a small folding knife. Ability: Appraise — can identify the value/purpose of unknown items in the inventory (reveals tooltips others can't see). Narrative anchor: she was second-in-command and Vane's betrayal is most personal to her.
Jin Park — The Navigator. Starts with a brass compass (cracked) and a torn star chart. Ability: Read the Sky — can interact with constellation puzzles and decode navigational ciphers. Narrative anchor: her brother was killed in the raid and she suspects Vane caused it.
Tomás "Spark" Reyes — The Powder Monkey / Engineer. Starts with flint, steel, and a bent nail. Ability: Tinker — can pick simple locks and disarm trap mechanisms, and is the only role who can safely handle gunpowder puzzles. Narrative anchor: youngest of the crew, treats the others as family.
Dr. Eliza Whitcombe — The Surgeon. Starts with a glass vial, a packet of dried herbs, and a leather journal of recipes. Ability: Alchemy — can mix two ingredients to create new items (poison, antidote, smoke, invisible-ink reveal). Narrative anchor: former Royal Navy surgeon who turned pirate after an injustice she's never explained.
Kwame Okafor — The Bo'sun. Starts with a length of rope and a brass whistle. Ability: Strength & Rigging — can force open jammed objects, lift heavy items, and is the only role who can solve knot/rigging puzzles. Narrative anchor: oldest of the group, the moral compass, knows sailor lore and superstition.
The roles are intentionally asymmetric. A puzzle that needs Alchemy will sit unsolved until Eliza arrives at it — meaning players have a real reason to talk constantly about what they're seeing.
Act Structure
The game has five acts, each set in a different area of the ship. The map is roughly: Brig (bottom) → Lower Decks → Upper Deck → Captain's Cabin → Helm (top). Earlier acts unlock the next, but Acts 2 and 3 contain parallel sub-areas players can split across.
Act 1 — The Brig (Target: 15 minutes)
The five wake in a single iron cell. The drunk jailor, Crooked Hal, is slumped at a desk across the room, snoring, with the keys on his belt — out of arm's reach. A single oil lantern swings overhead. Through the porthole, the moon is high; they have roughly 90 minutes until dawn.
The brig serves as the tutorial. It teaches inventory, role abilities, item combination, and the central tension: every player must contribute.
Puzzles:
The cell door has a heavy padlock that can be picked, but Tomás's bent nail is too short. Kwame's rope can be combined with Mira's folding knife (cut into a long strip) and tied around a hook on the cell ceiling, allowing him to swing and snag Hal's keyring — but only if Hal stays asleep. Hal wakes if loud noises are made. Eliza can identify that Hal's mug contains rum, and she has herbs that, brewed together, create a sleeping draught. The herbs need hot water — there is a candle stub on the lantern chain, but no way to keep it lit if removed. Jin can use her compass's metal back to focus the lantern's flame, heating water in Eliza's vial. The resulting draught is dripped (via the rope) into Hal's mug. He drinks, falls into deep sleep, and Kwame can now safely retrieve the keys.
This act teaches: combine items, share inventory, use role abilities, sequence actions across players. Everyone has at least one critical contribution. Even if a group skips ahead and tries to brute-force the lock, they'll discover Tomás's pick breaks without the proper procedure.
Exit: The cell unlocks. A stairwell leads up to the lower decks. As they ascend, the first ambient timer event triggers: footsteps overhead, and Vane's voice barking orders. The crew must now move stealthily — the alarm meter starts.
Act 2 — The Lower Decks (Target: 20 minutes)
A three-room sub-area: the Cargo Hold, the Galley, and the Powder Room. Players can (and should) split up here. Each room contains one piece of the puzzle that opens the door to the upper deck — but they are interlinked, so the groups must shout findings to each other.
Cargo Hold: Crates stacked against the stairwell to the deck. The crates have weight markings (some in stones, some in pounds, some in arrobas — Spanish loot). A balance scale on the wall must be loaded with crates totaling exactly the weight inscribed on the door's counterweight (a clue: the door says "By weight of gold alone shall ye pass"). Mira's Appraise ability reveals hidden weight markings on unmarked crates. The math is intentional but not punishing — there are multiple valid combinations.
Galley: Vane's recent meals are still on the table. A captain's log lies open. The log is written in a Caesar cipher shifted by 3 (a number Jin can deduce from a margin note: "the three brothers watch"). Decoded, it reads: "The bosun's whistle calls them. Three blasts, then two. Loyalists assemble on deck." This is critical for Act 3 — Kwame must learn this signal. There's also a locked spice cabinet. Eliza recognizes one spice jar contains belladonna, not nutmeg; using Alchemy she combines it with the rum bottle on the table to produce a tainted bottle (saved for Act 3).
Powder Room: A locked chest contains gunpowder cartridges and a brass key. The lock is a four-dial combination. The clue is a small framed sampler on the wall — a child's embroidery counting the Captain's "lucky days." Numbers don't immediately read out; players must notice the embroidered stars over four letters spelling a date that matches a ledger entry Mira holds. Tomás's Tinker ability is required to safely remove the gunpowder cartridges without sparks — needed in Act 5.
The act ends when the cargo weight is balanced and the stairwell opens. As they ascend, the alarm meter ticks up unless they took the tainted rum (which they can pour into a barrel marked "Watch Crew's Ration" on the way up, knocking out two of Vane's men silently).
Act 3 — The Upper Deck (Target: 20 minutes)
Open air. Stars. The ship's wheel ahead is manned by a loyalist named Garrett. Two more loyalists patrol the deck. The crew is exposed — the alarm meter rises faster here. Most of this act takes place in three sub-stations.
The Mast Station: The crow's nest above has a sealed flare pistol Vane intends to use to signal the Navy. Kwame must climb, using a rigging puzzle that requires identifying three knots (bowline, clove hitch, sheet bend) in the right order — illustrated on a tattered card Mira finds in Act 2.
The Bell Station: A ship's bell. Kwame, knowing the signal from the decoded log (three blasts then two), can ring it to summon the loyalists into a single cluster, where they can be ambushed — but only if the other puzzles are solved first. Ringing too early triggers a fight the crew will lose.
The Constellation Station: A celestial sphere on a pedestal, used by Vane to chart the ambush rendezvous. Jin's Read the Sky reveals the constellation pattern that aligns the sphere — and the pattern, when traced, draws a key shape. The shape matches a keyhole hidden on the Captain's Cabin door (Act 4).
The act resolves with the loyalists clustered, ambushed (a brief scripted scene rather than a combat puzzle — escape rooms don't need combat), and the wheel temporarily abandoned. Now the players have a choice: tie up Garrett (slower, safer) or knock him out (faster, but he might wake during Act 4 and re-trigger the alarm).
Act 4 — The Captain's Cabin (Target: 20 minutes)
The cabin is locked. The constellation-shaped key opens it. Inside: Vane's quarters. A desk piled with charts, a wall of books, a portrait of Vane himself, a globe, and a locked sea-chest beneath the cot.
This act is the densest, with several layered puzzles. It's also where the betrayal's full story is revealed through environmental clues.
The Book Wall: Pulling certain books in sequence opens a hidden compartment. The correct books are titled with words that, in order, form a verse from a sea shanty Kwame recognizes — but the verse is missing two words, which can only be found written on the inside of the captain's hat (hanging on a hook) and the bottom of a teacup (must be drained first; Eliza identifies it as cold, safe tea).
The Portrait: Tilts to reveal a wall safe with a five-symbol lock. The symbols correspond to the five crew tattoos described in Mira's ledger fragment from Act 1 — Vane has been tracking his crew and the safe's combination is the crew themselves. (Story beat: the realization that Vane has long planned this betrayal, with files on each of them.)
The Globe: The globe rotates and clicks. Aligning Port Royal with a small notch reveals a hollow chamber containing a folded letter — the Royal Navy's pardon offer to Vane, signed and dated. This is the evidence.
The Sea-Chest: A combination lock with three dials marked with ship names. The clue comes from the decoded captain's log (Galley, Act 2), the wall safe contents, and the constellation alignment — three independent pieces of information that the players already have if they've solved the previous acts. Inside the chest is the ledger (the win condition) and a brass key shaped like a kraken (needed for Act 5).
A complication: Garrett, if knocked out rather than tied, wakes during this act, and the alarm meter spikes. A timer appears at the top of the screen: "Loyalists alerted in 3:00." The players must finish the cabin before it expires, or face a forced encounter.
Act 5 — The Helm (Target: 15 minutes)
Back on deck. Dawn is breaking — the sky on the right side of the screen is visibly lightening. The Port Royal coastline is visible on the horizon. The Navy frigates are not yet in sight but will be within ten minutes of game time.
The wheel is locked by a brass mechanism shaped like a kraken — the key from the sea-chest fits, but the wheel itself is bound by ropes set up by Vane. Kwame must cut the ropes in a specific order (the rope-tension puzzle: cut the wrong one first and the whole rigging collapses, damaging the ship and starting a sinking timer).
Once the wheel turns, the final puzzle: plotting a course. Jin reads the stars and identifies a safe heading — but the course requires three corrections, each calling on information from earlier acts (a current direction from the captain's log, a reef location from the cargo hold's manifests, the rendezvous coordinates from the constellation sphere). The wheel is a three-dial mechanism; setting all three correctly sets the ship on course for the Spanish-controlled island of Hispaniola, where they can disappear.
Final beat: as the ship turns away from Port Royal, Vane emerges from belowdecks with a pistol. There's no combat — the resolution is a dialogue puzzle. Each crew member can present one piece of evidence (the ledger, the pardon letter, the captain's log entry, the tainted rum, the wall-safe files). Choosing the right item per player triggers a confession, and Vane is taken alive. Choosing wrong leads to a worse ending where one crew member is wounded but the ship still escapes.
The credits roll over a 2D animated sequence of the Vesper sailing into the sunrise — toward Hispaniola, not Port Royal.
Lock and Mechanism Types
For a 2D game, you want a variety of interaction types so puzzles feel distinct. I'd recommend implementing these as reusable systems:
Dial combination locks (2–5 dials, numbers or symbols). Use for chests, safes, doors.
Pattern-trace locks (draw a shape on a grid). Use for the constellation door, the wall safe.
Sequence locks (press objects in a specific order). Use for the book wall, the ship's bell.
Inventory combination (drag item A onto item B). Use for the alchemy system, the rope-and-knife rigging.
Weight/balance puzzles (load items onto a scale). Use for the cargo hold.
Cipher input (decode and type a word). Use for the captain's log.
Rope/wire routing (drag rope ends to correct anchors). Use for the rigging puzzles.
Lockpicking minigame (simple tension-and-pin, only Tomás can use). Use for the cell padlock and incidental locks.
Liquid mixing (drag two ingredients into a vial). Use for Eliza's alchemy.
Co-op locks (two or more players must interact simultaneously). Use for at least one puzzle per act after Act 1 — it forces continuous collaboration.
The co-op locks are the most important mechanic for a 5-player game. Suggestion: one in Act 2 (two players hold weights down while a third reads the door message), one in Act 3 (three players at three stations must time the bell, the climb, and the constellation alignment together), one in Act 4 (two players in the cabin while one watches the door), and the final wheel in Act 5 (three players, three dials, all set at once).
Hint System
A three-tier hint system works well. Hints should never solve a puzzle — they should restore a player to the state of "able to think productively" without spoiling the satisfaction.
Tier 1 — Nudge. A vague directional prompt. "Has anyone shown Eliza what's in the galley cabinet yet?" These are free but limited (5 per game). Delivered by a parrot companion named Salty, perched on a beam at the edge of the screen. Tapping him gives a context-aware nudge based on what the team has and hasn't tried in the last 60 seconds.
Tier 2 — Direction. A more specific suggestion. "The cipher in the log uses a simple letter shift. The margin note about three brothers might be the key." Costs the team 2 minutes off the timer. 3 available per game.
Tier 3 — Solution. Reveals the answer to one specific puzzle step. Costs 5 minutes and one of three "Captain's Mercy" tokens. Players are reluctant to spend these.
Additional ideas to layer in:
Role-specific Salty. When a puzzle requires a role's ability, the relevant player's screen briefly highlights — a soft hint that doesn't reveal the solution but tells the group who needs to be involved.
Stuck detector. If no progress has been made for 4 minutes, Salty offers a free Tier 1 hint unprompted ("Squawk! Try the galley!"). This protects players from grinding to a halt.
Whisper hints. Some hints arrive only to one player (the one whose role is relevant), which keeps the asymmetric-information feel intact.
Pacing and Tension Mechanics
The 90-minute timer is the master clock, but several sub-timers create texture:
Alarm meter (Acts 2–4): rises with mistakes, falls with stealthy actions like the tainted rum. If it maxes, a forced encounter triggers and costs the team 8 minutes.
Sunrise gradient. The sky background gradually lightens. By Act 5, this is the team's primary visual stress cue.
Garrett timer (Act 4): only triggers if players chose "knock out" over "tie up." A risk/reward decision in Act 3.
Sinking timer (Act 5): only triggers if the rope-cut puzzle is failed. Resets if Tomás can patch the damage with materials from the powder room.
These are all narrative timers, not arbitrary ones. Each tells a story about consequences of choices.
Replayability
A few hooks for replay value: branching endings (best, good, mediocre, bad — based on whether the ledger is recovered, whether Vane is taken alive, whether all crew survive), a Captain's Mercy speedrun mode (60 minutes, no hints), and randomized cipher keys / combination numbers each playthrough so that solutions can't be memorized — only the method carries over.
A New Game+ could let players switch roles, revealing different dialogue and inventory paths through the same scenario.
Suggested Build Order
If you're prototyping this, I'd build in this order:
Inventory system and item combination.
Role abilities as inventory tags / interaction filters.
Act 1 end-to-end as a vertical slice — it teaches every core system.
The hint system + Salty.
The co-op lock infrastructure (the hardest to retrofit).
Acts 2–5 in narrative order.
The branching ending logic and Vane confrontation last.
This way you have a playable tutorial loop in week one and can test the multiplayer feel before committing to the full content.
